---
term: FOB (Free on Board)
description: An incoterm where the seller's responsibility ends once the cargo is loaded on board the vessel at the port of origin.
---

## What is FOB?

FOB is one of the most commonly quoted [incoterms](/glossary/incoterms) in ocean freight. Under FOB, the seller handles and pays for everything up to and including loading the cargo onto the vessel at the named port of loading. From that point, the buyer takes on the cost and risk of ocean freight, insurance and import clearance.

## Key points

- The transfer point is specific: once the cargo is "on board," risk passes to the buyer — not when it leaves the seller's warehouse.
- The buyer typically arranges and pays for the ocean freight booking, so the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) often shows the buyer's nominated forwarder as the party paying the carrier.
- Common for buyers who want control over freight costs and carrier choice.
- Only suitable for sea or inland waterway transport — Incoterms has separate terms (like FCA) for other modes.

## Example scenario

A buyer in the US sources goods from a manufacturer in Malaysia on FOB Port Klang terms. The manufacturer delivers the goods to the port and loads them on board; from there, the buyer's own nominated freight forwarder handles the ocean freight, insurance and import formalities.
