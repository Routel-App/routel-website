## What are credit terms?

Credit terms (often written as "30-day terms" or similar) set how long a customer has to pay an invoice after it's issued, rather than paying cash on delivery. They're one of the main levers a forwarder uses to win and keep repeat business — and one of the main sources of cash-flow risk if extended carelessly.

## Key points

- Usually expressed in days from invoice date (7, 30, 60) and drives when an invoice shows up as overdue in [AR aging](/glossary/ar-aging).
- Often paired with a **credit limit** — a cap on total outstanding exposure, so terms don't translate into unlimited risk.
- Extending or raising credit is normally a deliberate approval step — a business applies, and the forwarder's finance team reviews and approves — rather than an automatic grant.
- An approved agreement typically has its own expiry and needs renewing, not an indefinite standing arrangement.

## Example scenario

A customer is granted 30-day credit terms with a USD 20,000 limit. Every invoice they're issued is due 30 days after the invoice date, and the forwarder's team watches their total open balance against that USD 20,000 ceiling before accepting new bookings for them.
