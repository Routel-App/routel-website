## What are Incoterms?

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms), published by the International Chamber of Commerce, are a short set of three-letter codes — like [FOB](/glossary/fob) or [CIF](/glossary/cif) — that both parties on a sales contract agree to, so there's no ambiguity about who pays for freight, insurance and customs at each leg, and at what point risk of loss transfers.

## Key points

- Without an agreed incoterm, "the price includes shipping" can mean very different things to a buyer and seller — incoterms remove that ambiguity.
- Common ones for ocean freight: EXW, FOB, CIF, CFR, DAP, DDP — each shifting the cost/risk line at a different point in the journey.
- The incoterm quoted also decides who's responsible for export and import customs clearance.
- Revised periodically (the current set is Incoterms 2020); contracts should state which version they're using.

## Example scenario

A buyer and seller agree a price "FOB Port Klang." The seller's responsibility — and the cost they've quoted — ends once the goods are loaded on board the vessel at Port Klang. From that point, ocean freight, insurance and import costs are the buyer's to arrange and pay for.
