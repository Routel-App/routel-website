---
term: CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)
description: An incoterm where the seller pays for ocean freight and insurance all the way to the destination port.
---

## What is CIF?

Under CIF, the seller's quoted price already includes the cost of the goods, ocean freight to the named destination port, and marine insurance covering the voyage. Risk of loss still transfers to the buyer once goods are loaded on board at origin — the seller is paying for freight and insurance on the buyer's behalf, not retaining the risk.

## Key points

- The seller books and pays the carrier, so the quoted price is simpler for the buyer to compare — freight is already included.
- Minimum insurance coverage is specified by the Incoterms rules; a buyer wanting more cover arranges it themselves.
- Once the goods are on board, the risk is the buyer's even though the seller is still paying the freight bill — a common point of confusion.
- Only applies to sea and inland waterway transport, like [FOB](/glossary/fob).

## Example scenario

A buyer wants a landed price without managing freight bookings themselves. The seller quotes CIF to the buyer's port, books the vessel, insures the cargo, and the buyer only has to handle import clearance and inland delivery once the goods arrive.
