## What is detention?

Once a container clears the port, the consignee typically has an agreed free period to unload it and return the empty box to the carrier's depot. Detention is what gets charged for every day past that free period the container is still out — at the consignee's warehouse, say — before it's returned empty.

## Key points

- Starts once the container leaves the port (picking up where [demurrage](/glossary/demurrage) leaves off, since the two don't usually overlap).
- Encourages fast unloading and return — carriers need their containers back in circulation for the next booking.
- Free time and daily rates are set per carrier and often negotiated as part of a shipping contract.
- Avoiding it is mostly about planning: booking the unloading slot before the container even arrives.

## Example scenario

A container is collected from the port on Monday with two free days to unload and return it. The warehouse doesn't get to unloading it until Thursday and doesn't return the empty box until Friday — three days past free time, billed as detention.
