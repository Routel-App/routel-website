## What is a shipper?

The shipper is the party who hands cargo to the carrier for transport — named on the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) as the sender. Like the [consignee](/glossary/consignee), the shipper of record isn't always the same company as the seller in the underlying trade; a seller can ship through an agent, who appears as shipper instead.

## Key points

- Responsible for providing accurate [Shipping Instructions](/glossary/shipping-instructions) — cargo description, weight, marks — before the carrier issues the B/L.
- Under [FOB](/glossary/fob) terms the shipper's obligations generally end once goods are loaded on board; under [CIF](/glossary/cif), the shipper also arranges freight and insurance.
- Usually the party responsible for export customs clearance, unless the agreed [incoterm](/glossary/incoterms) says otherwise.
- A freight forwarder consolidating several clients' cargo is sometimes itself named as shipper on the Master B/L, with each underlying client named on their own House B/L.

## Example scenario

A manufacturer sells goods FOB and hands the cargo to a carrier at the port, named as shipper on the resulting Bill of Lading. From that point, the buyer's nominated forwarder takes over arranging onward freight.
