## What is a Telex Release?

Normally, a consignee needs an original [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) in hand to collect cargo at destination. A telex release is the shipping line's electronic message to their destination agent confirming the originals have been surrendered at origin — so the consignee can collect the cargo without waiting for physical documents to arrive by courier.

## Key points

- Requested by the shipper (or the forwarder on their behalf) once payment terms allow it — often once the shipper has been paid or has no reason to hold the originals back.
- Speeds up collection, especially on short sea routes where the vessel can arrive before courier-mailed originals would.
- Once released, the original paper B/Ls are no longer valid for collecting the cargo — the telex release replaces them.
- Usually carries a small fee charged by the carrier for processing it.

## Example scenario

Cargo shipped on a three-day crossing would arrive before the original Bill of Lading could realistically be couriered to the consignee. The shipper requests a telex release once payment is confirmed, so the agent at destination can hand over the cargo the moment the vessel berths.
