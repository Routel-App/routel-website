## What is a consignee?

The consignee is who the cargo is going to — the party entitled to take delivery at destination, named on the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) or [Air Waybill](/glossary/awb). They aren't always the buyer in the underlying sale; a buyer can nominate a different consignee, such as their own customs broker or warehouse.

## Key points

- Named explicitly on the B/L or AWB, alongside the [shipper](/glossary/shipper) and often a separate [notify party](/glossary/notify-party).
- On a "to order" Bill of Lading, cargo release depends on presenting the original document rather than simply being the named consignee — see [Telex Release](/glossary/telex-release).
- Responsible for arranging import clearance and collection unless the shipping terms say otherwise.
- Routel's [public tracking page](/platform/tracking) checks a lookup against the consignee's company name or account number, so only the actual party to the shipment can see its status.

## Example scenario

A trading company buys goods and has them shipped, but nominates their appointed customs broker as the consignee on the B/L so the broker can clear and collect the cargo directly on the buyer's behalf.
