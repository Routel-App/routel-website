term: Freight Forwarder
description: A company that arranges the movement of cargo on behalf of shippers, without necessarily owning any transport itself.
---

## What is a freight forwarder?

A freight forwarder organizes the end-to-end movement of a shipment — booking space with carriers, arranging documentation, coordinating customs clearance and handling the handoffs between trucking, port and vessel — acting as the single point of contact for the shipper rather than the shipper dealing with every carrier directly.

## Key points

- Doesn't usually own ships, aircraft or trucks — the forwarder's value is coordination, rate negotiation and documentation, not owning assets.
- Can issue its own [House Bill of Lading](/glossary/house-bill-of-lading), consolidating multiple customers' cargo under one carrier booking.
- Often also handles customs brokerage, cargo insurance arrangements, and warehousing along the route.
- Makes money on the margin between what carriers charge and what's quoted to the shipper, plus service fees.

## Example scenario

An exporter needs cargo moved from a factory in Malaysia to a buyer's warehouse in the US — covering trucking to port, ocean freight, customs clearance at both ends, and final delivery. Rather than booking and coordinating all of that directly, the exporter hands the whole move to a freight forwarder, who manages every leg and hands over one Bill of Lading and one invoice.
