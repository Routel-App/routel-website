## What is a Master Bill of Lading?

The Master Bill of Lading (MBL) is issued by the ocean carrier — the shipping line — to whoever booked the container with them, which is often a freight forwarder rather than the end shipper. It governs the carrier's liability for the whole container.

## Key points

- Covers the carrier's side of the contract: carrier and forwarder (or NVOCC) as the named parties, not the underlying cargo owner.
- One MBL can sit above several [House Bills of Lading](/glossary/house-bill-of-lading) if the forwarder has consolidated multiple customers' cargo into one container.
- Only the carrier can amend or release an MBL — the forwarder manages the HBLs issued underneath it.
- Carries the carrier's own Master B/L number, separate from any House B/L number the forwarder assigns.

## Example scenario

A shipping line issues one Master B/L to a freight forwarder for a 40-foot container. The forwarder, who consolidated three separate customers' cargo into that same container, issues each of those three customers their own House B/L referencing the Master B/L's voyage details.
