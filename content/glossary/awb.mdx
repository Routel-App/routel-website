## What is an AWB?

An Air Waybill is issued by an airline (or a forwarder issuing a House AWB) when cargo is accepted for air transport. It serves the same receipt-and-contract role as an ocean [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading), with one key difference: an AWB is never a negotiable document of title, so cargo is released to whoever is named as consignee, not to whoever holds the original paper.

## Key points

- Issued as a Master AWB (airline to forwarder) and, where a forwarder consolidates cargo, a House AWB (forwarder to shipper) — the same master/house split as ocean freight.
- Not negotiable: unlike an ocean B/L, there's no "order" version — the named consignee collects the cargo, full stop.
- Covers one shipment per AWB number, used for tracking throughout the air leg.
- Typically moves faster through documentation than ocean freight, since air shipments are generally more time-sensitive.

## Example scenario

A buyer needs an urgent spare part flown in. The airline issues a Master AWB to the forwarder handling the booking, and because it's a single shipment with no consolidation, the forwarder's House AWB names the buyer directly as consignee for pickup at the destination airport.
