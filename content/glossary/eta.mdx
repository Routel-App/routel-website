## What is ETA?

ETA is the carrier's estimate of when a shipment will arrive at the destination port or airport. It's the single most-asked-about date on any shipment, and the one that drives a consignee's own planning — customs pre-clearance, trucking bookings, warehouse slots.

## Key points

- Set at booking from the carrier's published schedule and updated through the voyage as conditions change — weather, port congestion, transshipment delays.
- Paired with **ATA** (Actual Time of Arrival) once the vessel or flight actually arrives.
- Appears on the [public shipment tracking](/platform/tracking) status a forwarder's own customers can check without a login.
- Driving a quote or invoice timeline off ETA rather than ATA is a common source of friction when a shipment runs late — build in the gap.

## Example scenario

A consignee books inland trucking to collect cargo the same day the vessel's ETA says it will arrive. A two-day port delay means the actual arrival (ATA) slips past the original ETA — the trucking booking has to be rearranged, which is exactly the kind of update a shared tracking page is meant to surface early.
