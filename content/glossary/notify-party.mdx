## What is a notify party?

The notify party is named on the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) as who the carrier or their agent should contact when the cargo arrives at destination. It's often the same company as the [consignee](/glossary/consignee), but not always — a bank financing the shipment, a customs broker, or the buyer's logistics coordinator might be named instead so arrival notices reach the right desk.

## Key points

- A B/L can have more than one notify party if several people need the arrival alert.
- Getting it wrong means an arrival notice goes to the wrong inbox, which is exactly the kind of delay a shared status check prevents.
- Distinct from the consignee: the notify party doesn't necessarily have any legal right to the cargo, they're just who gets told it's arrived.
- Carried on both House and Master B/Ls when a shipment moves through a freight forwarder.

## Example scenario

A letter-of-credit shipment names the financing bank as notify party, so the bank is informed the cargo has arrived before releasing the original documents to the actual consignee for collection.
