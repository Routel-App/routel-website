---
term: LCL (Less than Container Load)
description: A shipment too small to fill a container by itself, consolidated with other shippers' cargo into one box.
---

## What is LCL?

LCL cargo shares a container with other shippers' goods, consolidated at a [CFS](/glossary/cfs) at origin and de-consolidated at another CFS at destination. It's the usual choice when a shipment doesn't justify booking a full container — see [FCL](/glossary/fcl).

## Key points

- Priced by volume (cubic metres) or weight, whichever is greater — see [Chargeable Weight](/glossary/chargeable-weight).
- Involves more handling than FCL: cargo is loaded into the container alongside other shipments, then unpacked and sorted again at the destination CFS.
- Transit can take a little longer than FCL, since the consolidator waits to fill the container before it moves.
- The forwarder issues a House Bill of Lading for the LCL portion, separate from the Master Bill of Lading covering the whole container — see [HBL](/glossary/house-bill-of-lading) and [MBL](/glossary/master-bill-of-lading).

## Example scenario

A buyer orders two pallets of goods — nowhere near enough to fill a container. The forwarder books it as LCL: the two pallets travel in a shared container with other shippers' cargo, and the buyer only pays for the space their two pallets actually use.
