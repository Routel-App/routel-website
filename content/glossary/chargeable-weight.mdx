term: Chargeable Weight
description: Whichever is greater between a shipment's actual weight and its volumetric weight — that's what freight is actually priced on.

## What is chargeable weight?

Light, bulky cargo takes up space on a vessel or aircraft out of proportion to how much it actually weighs. Chargeable weight solves that: carriers calculate a **volumetric weight** from the cargo's dimensions, compare it against the actual weight, and bill on whichever number is higher.

## Key points

- Matters most for [LCL](/glossary/lcl) ocean shipments and air freight, where space is the scarcer resource relative to weight.
- Volumetric weight formulas differ by mode — ocean freight typically converts 1 cubic metre to 1,000 kg; air freight commonly uses 1 cubic metre to 167 kg, though carriers vary.
- A shipment of foam packaging material might weigh very little but take up a lot of volume — it gets priced on volumetric weight, not actual weight.
- Always worth calculating both before quoting, since guessing wrong on a bulky-but-light shipment can quietly erase margin.

## Example scenario

A pallet of pillows weighs 80 kg but measures out to a volumetric weight of 220 kg. The carrier bills freight on 220 kg — the chargeable weight — not the 80 kg the pallet actually weighs on a scale.
