---
term: NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier)
description: A company that sells ocean freight and issues its own Bills of Lading, without owning or operating any ships.
---

## What is an NVOCC?

An NVOCC acts as a carrier to its customers — issuing Bills of Lading and taking on carrier liability — while actually buying space from real vessel-operating shipping lines to move the cargo. It sits between the shipper and the ocean carrier, similar in function to a freight forwarder but with the legal standing of a carrier.

## Key points

- Issues its own [House Bill of Lading](/glossary/house-bill-of-lading) to customers, backed by a [Master Bill of Lading](/glossary/master-bill-of-lading) from the actual vessel operator.
- Often buys container space in bulk at volume rates, then resells capacity to smaller shippers who couldn't get the same rate directly from a carrier.
- Regulated similarly to ocean carriers in many jurisdictions, since it takes on carrier liability for the cargo.
- A [freight forwarder](/glossary/freight-forwarder) can also register as an NVOCC to issue carrier-style documentation.

## Example scenario

A small exporter can't negotiate competitive rates directly with a major shipping line. An NVOCC, which buys large blocks of container space from several carriers, offers the exporter a better rate and issues its own House B/L for the shipment.
