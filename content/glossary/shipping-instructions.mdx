## What are Shipping Instructions?

Shipping Instructions (SI) are what the shipper (or their forwarder) submits to the carrier confirming the exact details to print on the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) — party names and addresses, cargo description, marks and numbers, freight terms. The carrier won't issue a B/L without it.

## Key points

- Submitted before the **SI cut-off**, a deadline set ahead of the vessel's departure so the carrier has time to prepare documentation before loading.
- Errors in the SI are one of the most common causes of B/L amendment fees and delays — it's worth a second pair of eyes before submission.
- Distinct from the booking itself: the booking reserves space and equipment; the SI tells the carrier what to print on the resulting document.
- Once submitted and the B/L is drafted, changes after the fact usually carry an amendment charge from the carrier.

## Example scenario

A booking is confirmed for a 20 October sailing with an SI cut-off of 17 October. The forwarder submits the consignee's exact legal name, cargo description and marks before that deadline so the carrier can draft and issue the Bill of Lading in time for the vessel to sail.
