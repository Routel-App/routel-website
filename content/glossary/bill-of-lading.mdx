## What is a Bill of Lading?

A Bill of Lading (B/L) does three jobs at once: it's a receipt for the cargo, evidence of the contract of carriage, and — for an "order" B/L — a document of title, meaning whoever holds the original can claim the goods at destination.

## Key points

- Issued by the carrier or their agent once cargo is received or loaded on board.
- Shows shipper, consignee, notify party, vessel and voyage, ports of loading and discharge, cargo description, weight and measurement.
- A shipment can have both a **House** B/L (issued by the freight forwarder to their customer) and a **Master** B/L (issued by the actual carrier to the forwarder) — see [HBL](/glossary/house-bill-of-lading) and [MBL](/glossary/master-bill-of-lading).
- Original B/Ls are often required at destination to release the cargo, unless a [Telex Release](/glossary/telex-release) has been arranged.

## Example scenario

A consignee can't clear their container at the port until they present an original Bill of Lading (or a telex release confirmation) to the shipping line's agent — it's the shipping industry's version of a claim ticket.
