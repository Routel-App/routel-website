---
term: House Bill of Lading (HBL)
description: The Bill of Lading a freight forwarder issues to their own customer, as opposed to the carrier's Master B/L.
---

## What is a House Bill of Lading?

When a freight forwarder books cargo with a carrier, the carrier issues a [Master Bill of Lading](/glossary/master-bill-of-lading) to the forwarder. The forwarder, in turn, issues their own **House** Bill of Lading to their actual customer — the shipper or consignee they have a direct commercial relationship with.

## Key points

- Same core information as any [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading) — parties, vessel, ports, cargo — but under the forwarder's own terms and conditions, not the carrier's.
- Lets a forwarder consolidate several customers' cargo under one Master B/L while still issuing each customer their own House B/L.
- Usually accompanied by a container or cargo annexure listing container numbers, seals and package counts.
- The forwarder is the one who can amend, reissue or release an HBL — not the ocean carrier.

## Example scenario

An exporter books through a freight forwarder rather than directly with a shipping line. The forwarder consolidates several clients' cargo into one container booked under a Master B/L, then issues the exporter their own House B/L covering just their shipment.
