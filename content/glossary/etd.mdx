term: ETD (Estimated Time of Departure)

## What is ETD?

ETD is the carrier's best estimate of when a vessel or flight will depart the port or airport of loading. It's what a booking confirmation is built around, and it's the baseline everyone — shipper, forwarder, consignee — plans the rest of the shipment's timeline from.

## Key points

- Set by the carrier at booking and updated if the schedule changes — vessels roll, flights get delayed, cut-offs shift.
- Paired with **ATD** (Actual Time of Departure) once the vessel or flight actually leaves — the two can differ.
- Works upstream of cut-off dates: cargo and documentation cut-offs are set relative to the ETD, not the other way around.
- Appears on the booking confirmation and flows onto the booking and the [Bill of Lading](/glossary/bill-of-lading).

## Example scenario

A booking confirmation shows an ETD of 12 October. A rollover a few days before sailing pushes the actual departure to 15 October — the ETD on the booking gets updated, and everyone downstream (customer updates, invoicing timing) adjusts to the new date rather than the original estimate.
