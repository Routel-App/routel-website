---
term: CFS (Container Freight Station)
description: A warehouse near the port where LCL cargo from different shippers is consolidated into, or unpacked out of, shared containers.
---

## What is a CFS?

A Container Freight Station is where [LCL](/glossary/lcl) cargo physically changes hands between "loose cargo" and "cargo inside a shared container." At origin, several shippers' goods are consolidated into one container at the CFS; at destination, that container is unpacked (de-consolidated) and each consignee's portion is sorted out again.

## Key points

- Sits near the port, since cargo moves between the CFS and the vessel as part of the same handling chain.
- The point where LCL cargo is physically inspected, weighed and measured before it's loaded.
- Storage and handling fees at a CFS are usually billed separately from ocean freight itself.
- FCL cargo generally bypasses a CFS entirely, since the whole container already belongs to one shipper.

## Example scenario

Three different exporters each have a half-container's worth of cargo heading to the same destination port. A forwarder consolidates all three shipments into one container at the origin CFS; at the destination CFS, the container is unpacked and each consignee's goods are separated out for their own collection.
