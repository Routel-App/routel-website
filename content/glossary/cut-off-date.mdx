term: Cut-off Date
description: The deadline for getting cargo, documents or instructions to the carrier before a vessel or flight departs.

## What is a cut-off date?

A cut-off is a hard deadline the carrier sets ahead of departure — miss it, and the shipment doesn't make that sailing or flight, full stop. A single booking can carry several different cut-offs for different things.

## Key points

- **Cargo cut-off**: the last time physical cargo can be delivered to the port or terminal for loading.
- **SI cut-off**: the deadline for submitting [Shipping Instructions](/glossary/shipping-instructions) so the carrier can prepare the Bill of Lading in time.
- **VGM cut-off**: the deadline for submitting the container's verified gross mass, a regulatory requirement before loading.
- Each cut-off sits a different number of days before the vessel's [ETD](/glossary/etd) — missing any one of them can bump a shipment to the next available sailing.

## Example scenario

A booking has a cargo cut-off of Thursday and an SI cut-off of Friday, for a vessel departing the following Monday. Cargo delivered Friday morning — after the cargo cut-off — gets rolled to the next available sailing, even though the vessel hasn't left yet.
