term: AR Aging (Accounts Receivable Aging)
description: A report grouping unpaid customer invoices by how overdue they are, so collection effort goes where it matters.
---

## What is AR aging?

AR aging takes every open customer invoice and buckets it by how long it's been outstanding past its due date — typically current, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, and 60+ days overdue. It's the standard way a finance team prioritizes who to chase first.

## Key points

- Buckets get worse the longer an invoice sits unpaid — a 60+ day bucket is a bigger red flag than a 1–30 day one.
- Usually run per customer, so a finance team can see total exposure to one account across several overdue invoices at once.
- Its mirror image is **AP aging** — the same bucketing applied to what you owe vendors, instead of what customers owe you.
- A healthy aging report skews heavily toward "current," with little sitting in the oldest buckets.

## Example scenario

A finance manager runs AR aging at month end and finds one customer has USD 8,000 sitting in the 60+ day bucket across two invoices. That's the account collections calls that week, rather than chasing a handful of invoices only a few days past due.
