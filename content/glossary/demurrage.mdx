## What is demurrage?

Demurrage is what a carrier charges when a consignee doesn't collect a container from the port (or terminal) within the agreed free period. It applies while the container is still inside the port or terminal, waiting to be picked up — once it leaves, a related but separate charge, [detention](/glossary/detention), can start applying instead.

## Key points

- Free time is agreed per carrier and port — commonly a handful of calendar days after discharge.
- Charges usually escalate the longer the container sits — a flat daily rate that increases the further past free time it goes.
- Caused by delays in customs clearance, documentation, payment, or simply slow pickup — not a shipping-line service fee.
- Distinct from [detention](/glossary/detention), which covers the time the empty container spends outside the port after pickup, before it's returned.

## Example scenario

A container is discharged from the vessel on Monday with three days of free time. Customs clearance isn't completed until Friday, so the container sits at the port two days past its free time — the consignee is billed demurrage for those two extra days.
