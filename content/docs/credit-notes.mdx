Only draft invoices can be edited. Sometimes the amount on an issued invoice needs to change — a short shipment, a billing error, an extra charge discovered after the fact. Credit and debit notes make that adjustment formal instead of editing the original invoice.

## Credit vs. debit

<Fields
  rows={[
    ["Credit note", "Reduces what the customer owes — a refund, a discount, a correction in their favor."],
    ["Debit note", "Increases what the customer owes — an extra charge discovered after the invoice was issued."],
  ]}
/>

## Lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft">
    Choose the invoice it relates to, the amount, and a reason. The currency must match the invoice's currency.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Numbered">
    Finalizing assigns the note a real, sequential number — same discipline as finalizing an invoice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Applied">
    The note is applied against that specific invoice, adjusting the balance owed. A note can only be applied to an
    invoice that is already **finalized** — not a draft.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The original invoice itself is never edited after the fact; the note sits alongside it so the audit trail shows exactly what changed and why.

## Where it shows up

Applied notes appear on the customer's statement of account and in AR aging, netted against the invoice they adjust — see [Business Partners](/docs/business-partners) and [Accounting Reports](/docs/accounting).

## Through the assistant

"Issue a credit note on INV-2026-0183 for USD 150, short-shipped one carton" proposes a drafted note against that invoice for you to review and confirm.
