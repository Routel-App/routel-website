Routel keeps two records apart: the **route rate**, what a vendor charges you for a lane, maintained by **Pricing**, and the **quotation**, what you charge the customer for it, built by **Sales** on top of a current rate. Keeping both in the system means a quote is never built on an expired price, and an accepted quote turns into a booking without anyone re-typing it.

## Routes

**Routes** in the menu is where pricing and performance for every shipping route live, on two tabs: **Route Rates** and **Route Analytics**.

### Route Rates

A route rate is one vendor's buying price for one lane (POL → POD) and one container type, valid for a period.

#### Keeping rates current

Rates change often. Routel does not delete expired rates; it keeps them for history and stops offering them when you build a quotation. Rates about to lapse show as **Expiring**, so Pricing can chase a refreshed rate before it runs out, not after a quotation fails. When a vendor sends a new rate sheet, add rates one by one with **New Rate** or **Import from Excel** to load the whole sheet in one go. If a vendor simply holds a price for longer, **Extend validity** instead of re-entering it. Old and new can coexist, and the quotation screen only shows what is valid on the day.

### Route Analytics

The **Route Analytics** tab shows how your lanes actually perform: total shipments, unique routes, top routes by volume, top origins and destinations, monthly volume, and revenue, cost, profit and margin. Use it to see which lanes are worth pricing more aggressively, and which earn less than they look.

</Callout>

## Quotations

A quotation is issued to a customer for a route and equipment, priced on top of a selected buying rate.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the customer">
    Customer details, payment terms and credit limit come from the partner record. See [Business Partners](/docs/business-partners).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the lane and equipment">
    Origin, destination and container type. Routel lists the route rates that are valid for that combination.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a buying rate and set the selling price">
    Pick the vendor rate you intend to use. Enter the selling price per container or as a total; the expected margin is
    shown as you type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add other charges">
    Terminal handling, documentation, haulage, customs or any line the customer will be billed for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the quote validity and send">
    The quotation is numbered and rendered as a PDF for the customer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Quotation status

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Being prepared. Not yet sent. |
| Sent | Issued to the customer. |
| Accepted | Customer agreed. Ready to convert. |
| Declined | Customer said no. Kept for the record. |
| Expired | Validity date passed without acceptance. |

## Converting a quotation into a booking

Open an accepted quotation and choose **Convert to Booking**. Routel creates the booking with the customer, route, equipment and the quotation's charges carried across. If the quotation offered several routes, you pick the one the customer accepted, and only that route's charges are copied. Operations then completes the carrier booking, containers and B/L. See [Bookings](/docs/bookings).

The quotation becomes read-only, with a **Converted** badge and a link to the booking, so anyone can see what was promised against what was billed.

## Through the assistant

"Quote Al Noor Trading for a 40HC Port Klang to Jeddah using the Maersk rate" produces a proposed quotation with the valid rate selected and the customer's usual charges added. Confirm to issue it.

</Callout>
