Routel's finance module is built around one idea: **an invoice, a payment or a note is a transaction, and transactions post to the ledger the moment they are saved.** There is no separate "post to accounts" step and no month-end batch. Everything you read in [Accounting Reports](/docs/accounting) is derived live from the entries described on this page.

## Customer invoices

An invoice is raised against a job. Line items usually come from the job's selling price (ocean freight, terminal handling, documentation and so on), but any line can be added or edited before the invoice is issued.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the job and choose Create invoice">
    The customer and currency default from the job. Lines are prefilled from the selling price.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the lines">
    Adjust descriptions, quantities and prices. Add lines for extras that were agreed after quotation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Issue">
    The invoice is numbered, posted to Accounts Receivable and revenue, and the PDF is generated.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Through the assistant">
  "Create an invoice for JOB-2026-0412 in MYR" produces a proposed invoice with the same prefilled lines. Nothing is
  issued until you confirm. See [AI Assistant](/docs/ai-assistant).
</Callout>

## Payments

Record a payment against one or more invoices. Partial payments are supported; the invoice moves to *Partially paid* and the balance stays in aging. Payments post to the bank or cash account you select and clear the receivable.

## Credit notes and debit notes

- A **credit note** reduces what a customer owes: a pricing correction, a waived charge, a returned deposit. It can be applied to an open invoice or left on account.
- A **debit note** increases what a customer owes for charges that arose after invoicing, such as demurrage or a documentation amendment.

Both are numbered, rendered as PDF, and post to the ledger like an invoice.

## Credit limits and credit applications

Customers can be given a **credit limit**. The job page and the invoice screen show current exposure against the limit, so Operations sees the situation before accepting a new shipment.

A **credit application** is the record of a customer requesting or increasing a limit. Accounting reviews it, and the approved limit takes effect on the customer record.

<Callout kind="permission">
  **Accounting** and **Admin** issue invoices, record payments and notes, and approve credit applications.
  **Management** can view everything on this page. **Sales** can see invoice status for their customers.
</Callout>

## Receivables & Payables hub

One screen for what is owed to you and what you owe:

- **Receivables**: open customer invoices, sorted by due date, with aging buckets and quick actions to record a payment or issue a note.
- **Payables**: vendor costs recorded on jobs that are not yet paid, grouped by vendor.

From here you can also generate a customer's or vendor's **statement of account** as a PDF. See [Vendors & Customers](/docs/partners).

## Multi-currency behaviour

| Situation | What Routel does |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice in USD | Presented and paid in USD. Ledger records USD amount and MYR equivalent at the invoice date rate. |
| Vendor cost in USD on an MYR job | Converted at the exchange rate recorded on the job, so gross profit is computed in one currency. |
| Exchange differences | Recorded as a *Currency exchange* expense or gain when the settlement rate differs. See [Expenses & Payroll](/docs/expenses). |

## What posts where

| Action | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issue invoice | Accounts Receivable | Revenue |
| Record payment | Bank / Cash | Accounts Receivable |
| Credit note | Revenue | Accounts Receivable |
| Debit note | Accounts Receivable | Revenue |
| Record vendor cost | Cost of Sales | Accounts Payable |

You never enter these journals yourself. They are shown here so you know what the General Ledger will contain.
