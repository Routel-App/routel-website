Extending trade credit to a customer — letting them pay on terms instead of upfront — goes through a formal application rather than someone quietly raising a limit on their record.

## The flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Business applies">
    A public form captures the business's details and the credit amount they're requesting. No login needed to
    apply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Staff review">
    The application lands with staff to review — who the business is, what they're asking for, and against what
    existing relationship if any.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve or reject">
    Approving or rejecting is a deliberate decision, not a default. There's no silent auto-approval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agreement takes effect">
    An approved application becomes an active credit agreement tied to that customer's [Business Partner](/docs/business-partners)
    record: a limit, a currency, a term, and an expiry date.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note">
  Approving a new application for a customer automatically retires whatever credit agreement they had before — a
  customer only ever has one active agreement at a time.
</Callout>

## What the agreement controls

Once active, the agreement's limit and currency are what [Business Partners](/docs/business-partners) and booking/invoice creation check against when they show exposure — the same limit mechanics described there, just sourced from an approved application instead of a free-text field.

## Through the assistant

"Show me pending credit applications" or "What's Al Noor's current credit limit and when does it expire?" both work as immediate answers.

<Callout kind="permission">
  **Admin**, **Management** and **Accounting** review, approve and reject credit applications.
</Callout>
